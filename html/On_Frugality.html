On Frugality

$$$

Mitanshu Sukhwani

2026-05-01

Recently I came across a blog post, talking about Maladaptive Frugality, on Hacker News. The post talks about how when the author needed their iPhone repaired, they procrastinated bearing the expense and drained themselves about it. This feeling hits a little too close to home.

Coming from a Sindhi lineage, we are the shining beacon of frugality. We even pride ourselves in being resourceful, and why shouldn’t that be the case? Most of the Sindhi’s you see in India, and elsewhere, are refugees who ran away from Pakistan during the partition. My maternal grandfather started a tea business on a lorry when he was thrown from the train to a random station in India. He worked hard, and made a name for himself in the community of that small town, where he established himself a successful clothing shop. You’ll find stories of perseverance ingrained in our culture from the way we live to the food we make. Dimple Senani exhibits this through her culinary series on Beyond Dal Pakwaan. Funnily enough, I got to know more about my history from this youtube video by Mohak Mangal than my own family.

I am genuinely amazed by the inventiveness of my ancestors in difficult times, and find their grit to be absolutely inspiring. It is because of my parents’ hardwork that I can pursue my interests without worrying about putting the food on the table. But why do I have to carry the same (deeply instilled) frugal mindset, to the point where it is detrimental to my mental health? Why is it that I risked booking my flight tickets through an obviously shady looking website just to save a few bucks? (In my defense, ly.com turned out legit and I did read some reddit reviews before making the call.) Heck, I’ve seen my sindhi friends go through the same turmoil. While my friend is working in Google and earns a decent chunk, she pained herself over the doctor’s consultation charges for a serious injury.

My girlfriend, coming from a different background, isn’t so afraid to spend on things that matter. And it is only after her nudges, I even started to register the issue. I should definitely get those laundry clips to hang dry my clothes so they don’t fall away in the wind. And then I should also look into getting some furniture for my apartment. Till then, I hope you realise that it is okay to spend money, and make a conscious effort to not fall into the trap.
