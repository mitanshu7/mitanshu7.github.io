Filter Coffee Recipe

Thicc decoction

Mitanshu Sukhwani

2026-01-12

My dear friend (Who you may know from What about the Island?) asked me for my south indian filter coffee recipe, so I took the chance to record it here for posterity and you fine folks of the Internet. It yields 2 to 3 cups.

Ingredients:

  • A South Indian Filter.

  • 15 gms of filter coffee (80% coffee, 20% chicory).

    • Parampare and Bayars have yielded very good results for me.

  • 60 gms hot water (4x the weight of coffee).

    • I bring the water to a boil and then wait for a couple of minutes to let it cool down.
    • Try cooling the water a bit more if you get bitter brews.

  • 200 gms of whole milk (4.5% to 6% fat).

  • Sugar to taste.

    • I use a 1:10 ratio of sugar:milk.
    • Meaning, you would add 10 gms of sugar to 100gms of milk.

Recipe:

  1. Take the upper chamber (one with a lot of holes in the bottom) and place it on top of the cap. This helps retain any loose grounds that may fall from the holes.

  2. Now put your weighed coffee in the upper chamber.

  3. Shake the brewer left and right to level the bed of coffee.

  4. Remove the cap from the bottom and place it aside. You may even insert back the collected coffee in the cap.

  5. Merge the top and the bottom chambers.

  6. Pour 30 gms (2x coffee weight) of hot water, over the bed of coffee, in circular motion. Make sure to evenly wet the bed and not leave any dry pockets of coffee.

  7. Wait for 1 minute to let the coffee bloom.

  8. Now pour the remaining 30 gms (2x coffee weight) of hot water, again, in circular motion.

  9. I like to gently swirl the brewer after the final pour to level the bed of coffee.

  10. You can now cap the brewer, but take care to not seal it tight. Otherwise, the brew may stall as there might not be any airflow. I leave a little slit open. The decoction should be ready in 10 minutes.

  11. Heat the milk—adding sugar if you prefer—and bring it to a boil.

  12. Here is where you control the strength of your coffee. I serve a 1:10 ratio to guests. (10 gms decoction to 100gms of milk.) However, I personally prefer a 2:10 ratio.

Notes:

  • I would expect the decoction weigh to near 2x the weight of coffee. Your typical “espresso strength”.

  • Did you spot how conveniently I neglected the umbrella that comes with the brewer? Well, don’t use it. The shape tends to be a dome, rather than a flat disc, and distorts how evenly you water the coffee.

  • Talking about even water flow, I place the Aeropress Cap on top of the brewer and then pour the water.

  • Some people may not like the swirling part, but they haven’t tasted my brew yet. :D

  • I have been using a brewer with an opening in the bottom chamber. This highly reduces the chances of the brew stalling. However, you may get away with a run of the mill version.

  • You can scale up or scale down the recipe, but it’s generally hard to get good results from 10gm of filter coffee.
