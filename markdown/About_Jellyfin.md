There is one question that I get asked a lot. How do my partner and I manage a Long Distance Relationship (LDR)? I always point to communication. 

This realisation was not natural or easy for me. Things are much simpler when you both live in the same house/city/state/country/timezone. It is a whole different ballgame when the timezones are flipped. And do not get me started on [Daylight Saving Time][1]. What do you mean your intricately tuned circadian rhythm to match the other person's timezone for maximum overlap is now thrown off by an hour?

Given that I was a very shy person, it was impossible to get me on a call. This resulted in my partner feeling left out to the point where we had to go on a break till I fixed these issues. What do we say? ['Absence makes the heart grow fonder'][2] 

Heck, I think I was just a bad communicator. Imagine this, there is a person that loves you, on the other side of the world, with no idea of [the minutiae of your everyday life][3]. What do you do? Start slow. Just let them know how you feel today, what food you had, what piqued your interest, what was bulls*t. And then ask them the same things in return. Really listen to them as they picture their day for you, and put yourself in their shoes as they are doing so. How would you have felt if something they describe happened with you? Then tell them how you feel about it!

At this point I might sound very obvious. Aren't I just talking about talking? Duh! That's exactly what you do. Day in, Day out. You can even scale the medium of your conversations at your own pace. Start with text, then have a voice call over the weekend, then everynight. Slowly and steadily you'll come to crave video calls from them daily. This of course presumes the more you know them, you come to like them even more as a person!

Anyway, wasn't I going to tell you something about [Jellyfin][4]? Oh yeah, so assuming you already have a [selfhosting setup][5], you can now stream movies/shows/music/books. It's your own personal Netflix of sorts. We hop onto a [Google Meet][6], where they share their screen, play the media, and [enjoy our drinks][7].

I have saved the [instructions for running jellyfin on podman](jellyfin_podman_instruction_20260912.html), just for posterity. But you should always go to their [latest documentation][8]. It works on a client-server model, where the computer running 24x7 hosts all the compute, disks, and media to serve to the clients. Once the server is setup and running, grab your copy of the [Jellyfin Client](https://jellyfin.org/downloads/clients/) for [Desktop](https://github.com/jellyfin/jellyfin-desktop/releases), [Android](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=org.jellyfin.mobile), [iOS](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/jellyfin-mobile/id1480192618), [Fire TV](https://www.amazon.com/gp/aw/d/B07TX7Z725), or [Samsung TV](https://www.samsung.com/us/tvs/smart-tv/samsung-tv-apps-detail/?appId=3202007021208)!

If you do end up using Jellyfin—and love it like I do—I highly encourage you to [contribute to their project](https://jellyfin.org/contribute/) (however you can) or [donate on OpenCollective](https://opencollective.com/jellyfin)!
